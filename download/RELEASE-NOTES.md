**RipScout BBS 2.7.0: Installer, Internet Check, easier-to-read menus and classic RIP art**

- **New installer (RipscoutBBS-Setup.exe):** installs to `C:\RipScoutBBS` by default (or any folder you pick). Everything the board uses (the program, members, messages, files, settings) lives in that one folder, so moving the board to another PC is just copying the folder. The installer adds the Windows Firewall rule, Start menu and desktop shortcuts, and an optional "start with Windows". Uninstalling keeps your board's data unless you tick the box to remove it. Already running a board? You don't need the installer: the built-in updater keeps doing its job.
- **Internet Check (Control Panel > Dashboard):** tests whether callers can really reach your board: it's answering calls, Windows Firewall allows it, this PC is online, and an outside checker calls your public address and confirms it's your board that answers (plus the chat link and finger ports). It shows a big green **All tests good - live on the internet**, or exactly what's wrong and how to fix it, with a **Fix** button for the firewall rule. It runs at start, every 6 hours, after you change ports, and whenever you press **Run Check**. The tray icon's tooltip shows the result too.
- **Easier-to-read menus:** menu buttons now use the sharp screen font instead of thin drawn letters. Prefer the old look? Settings > **Menu button lettering** > Classic (live).
- **Classic RIP art pack:** Control Panel > Files > **Add the classic RIP art pack** (also a checkbox in first-run setup) adds 88 RIPscrip pictures by ACiD, iCE, Fire and others to a **Classic RIP Art** library, each with its title, artist, group and year. They appear in RIP Showcase > Art Gallery right away. The art belongs to its artists, so it isn't inside RipScout: the board fetches it from the 16colo.rs archive and checks every file's fingerprint. Pieces with slurs, profanity or nudity are left out.
- **Art Gallery:** a picture that's in two libraries is listed once.
- **Public host name** setting (Settings) for the "how to call in" address, used by the Internet Check.
